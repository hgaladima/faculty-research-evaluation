# Provisional Scoring Policy: Thresholds and Criteria Applied

Prepared 10 October 2026 for Phase 2 of the faculty research evaluation tool.

**Status: provisional.** Nothing in this note is School policy. Most open items in `REVIEW_FINDINGS.md` need a decision from the annual review committee. So that a working tool can be shown now, each item was given an interim default drawn from the Old Dominion University Faculty Handbook and from practice at other American universities. Every default can be changed on the app's Scoring policy tab, and the original Phase 1 rules remain available there as a preset.

Finding numbers (for example 2.5) refer to `REVIEW_FINDINGS.md`.

## 1. What changed, in brief

Nineteen rules differ from Phase 1. The model itself is unchanged: three domains weighted 50 / 35 / 15, 70% of a domain's points for meeting its minimum, and the same unit values.

Under the provisional policy the 22 test profiles classify as 2 Exceeds, 15 Meets and 5 Does Not Meet (Phase 1: 5, 13 and 4). Four profiles change class; section 5 explains each.

## 3. Rules applied

Each row is a setting on the Scoring policy tab.

### Classification

| Finding | Provisional rule | Phase 1 | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Meets requires every minimum **and** a score of at least 70 | Minimums alone decided Meets | Makes the published threshold real. No effect under the default numbers, where meeting every minimum earns exactly 70; it protects against settings that break that link |
| 2.3 | The presentation minimum needs the faculty member as presenter, or as mentor of a student presenter. A non-presenting coauthor earns half the minimum's points | Any role counted | Boise State: "presented at a conference". The Working Group lists "co-author on presentation but not presenting" as a lesser activity |
| 2.4 | A new award meets the grant minimum only in a PI, MPI, Co-PI or Co-I role. Internal submissions still count | Any role on any award counted | Handbook B.3: diligence in seeking "extramural or intramural funding" and leadership on the grant |
| 2.5 | Exceeds needs a score of at least 85 **and** at least two domains at 80% of their points | Score alone | Oklahoma: excellence in two areas. Boise State: Exceeds requires Meets plus several further items. Stops Exceeds on publication volume alone |
| 2.6 | The large-award route to Exceeds needs a score of at least 80 | No floor (Exceeds was possible at 75.2) | Judgement: the award should sit on top of a solid year |
| 2.7 | One unmet minimum with a score of at least 60 is labelled "approaching expectations" for committee attention. The class is still Does Not Meet | No such label | Four- and five-level scales are common (UTEP, Kansas State); the Phase 1 brief fixes three classes, so this is a label, not a fourth class |

### Adjustments

| Finding | Provisional rule | Phase 1 | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | At half the standard expectation or less, the grant and presentation minimums are met by documented activity (for example a proposal in development, a seminar, a collaborator role). Nothing is waived outright, and the report states which allowance was used | Waived silently below half; no relief at exactly half | Kansas State: expectations "prorated for actual time allocated". Removes the 19% versus 20% cliff |
| 3.2 | A faculty member with 0% research effort or 0 active months is "Not Evaluated" and referred to the committee | Rated on a floor factor of 0.25 | Handbook: research is expected of tenured and tenure-track faculty and recognised "where appropriate" for others |
| 3.6 | The three-year rolling window can rescue one domain a year | All three at once | Boise State and Arizona both smooth publications over two years, not every domain |
| 3.6 | The window compares this year's minimum plus the standard minimum for the two prior years | This year's (possibly prorated) minimum for all three years | A leave year should not lower the bar for earlier years |

### Counting

| Finding | Provisional rule | Phase 1 | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | A proposal funded in the year it was submitted earns its credit as an award only. Submitting it still counts toward the grant minimum | Credited twice | Phase 1 brief: avoid double counting |
| 4.2 | A DOI entered twice counts once; the entry that earns the most credit is the one kept, whatever the order | Counted twice | As above |
| 4.2 | DOIs and grant ids are matched ignoring capital letters, spaces and web-address prefixes such as `https://doi.org/` | Compared exactly as typed | Judgement: the same item typed two ways is still one item |
| 4.3 | A publication can be marked "credited in a prior year" and is then excluded | No guard | As above. An item counts in the year it first appears, online or in print |
| 4.6 | Book-chapter credit is capped at 2.25 units (three chapters) | No cap | Judgement: Boise State treats two chapters as one item |
| 4.6, 5 | Published abstracts earn 0.2 units; non-peer-reviewed books 1.0 and chapters 0.4; all inside the existing cap of 2.0 on other outputs. Any unrecognised type is flagged | Zero, silently | Working Group lists published abstracts. Handbook B.1 grades books by publisher and review |
| 4.7 | Second author earns 0.85 of lead credit. Sole, first, senior (last) and corresponding authors are all "lead" | Lead 1.0 or coauthor 0.7 only | Handbook B.1: "sole, first, or second author". Working Group: "primary, second, or senior". Arizona: first, second, third or senior |

### Missing data and rounding

| Finding | Provisional rule | Phase 1 | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.5 | Anything not yet verified is treated the least generous way and flagged: journal Tier 3, venue Tier C, presentation role coauthor, book role coauthor, grant role collaborator | Mixed: some defaults generous, most unflagged | Consistency |
| 6.6 | Scores round half up (the Excel calculator can only round this way). The overall score is the rounded true total; where the three displayed domain scores would not add up to it, one of them is shown 0.1 higher or lower | Rounded separately, so parts did not always add up | Half-up is the rule Excel's ROUND uses; the report must add up, and meeting every minimum must show exactly 70 under any weights |

## 4. Definitions applied

These are written into the app's help text. They are proposals.

**Journal tier.** A proxy, not a measure of an individual article; the committee may reassign a tier with a stated reason.

| Tier | Definition |
|---|---|
| 1 | Top quartile (Q1) of its subject category in Journal Citation Reports or Scimago, or the flagship journal of a major professional society in the field |
| 2 | Second quartile (Q2) |
| 3 | Any other peer-reviewed journal indexed in MEDLINE, Scopus or Web of Science |
| 0 | No verifiable peer review, or on a list the committee treats as predatory. Earns nothing and does not count toward the minimum |

**Venue tier.** Quality and selectivity count more than geography.

| Tier | Definition |
|---|---|
| A | Major national or international meeting of a recognised society, with competitive peer-reviewed abstract selection |
| B | Regional or state meeting of a recognised society, or a national meeting with light review |
| C | Local meeting, or limited review |
| 0 | Questionable academic credibility. Earns nothing |

**Award size.** The total amount for the full project period. Under $10,000 is minor (Working Group). $250,000 or more is large; the line is the engine's own and happens to match the NIH modular budget limit, which is a per-year figure. Internal funding is one band whatever its amount.

**Active months.** Subtract approved medical, parental and other non-research leave. Count sabbatical and research leave as active.

**Evidence.** Proposals in development, unfunded projects, manuscripts under review and submitted abstracts are entered as counts. The tool cannot check them; the committee should require documentation.

**Recorded, not scored.** Research awards, mentoring and dissertation supervision, community-engaged and translational work, journal quartile, impact factor and conference scope are captured and printed on the report, and do not change the score.

## 5. Effect on the 22 test profiles

| Profile | Phase 1 | Provisional | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| S2 High publications, limited grants | 86.1 Exceeds | 86.1 Meets | Only Publications is at 80% or more (Grants 70%, Presentations 78%) |
| S9 Co-I on several large grants | 80.2 Meets | 75.0 Does Not Meet | The only presentation is a poster as non-presenting coauthor, so the presentation minimum is unmet. Flagged "approaching" |
| S12 Strong research mentorship | 85.3 Exceeds | 85.3 Meets | Only Publications is at 80% or more |
| S14 Policy-impact research | 88.0 Exceeds | 88.0 Meets | Only Publications is at 80% or more (Grants 78%) |

The other 18 keep their class. Rounding half up moves one overall score by 0.1 (S4, from 83.3 to 83.4). In S4, S12 and S14 one displayed domain score is shifted by 0.1 so that the three add up to the overall score.

S14 shows the cost of the two-domain rule: a strong publication year with a funded major award as PI falls just short. Lowering the "strong domain" level from 80% to 75% would return S14 to Exceeds. That is a committee call.

## 6. Left as in Phase 1

| Finding | Why it was not changed |
|---|---|
| 1.1 Minimums (two publications; one grant activity; one presentation) | No source found for different numbers. Arizona's "two to three papers per year" is consistent with two |
| 1.5 Standard research effort of 40% | **The number most in need of confirmation.** ODU sets workload in load hours, not percentages. 40/40/20 is a common norm elsewhere. One ODU college's published workload gives research-active faculty 6 of 24 load hours for research, which is 25%. Every prorated expectation depends on this value |
| 2.8 Unit values for unfunded and funded grant activity | The Working Group asks that unfunded effort be recognised |
| 3.3, 3.4 Cap on the adjustment factor at 1.5; stepwise publication minimum | No basis found for an alternative |
| 3.5 Rank, tenure status, discipline, transition between fields | Not modelled. Left to the committee and the narrative |
| 4.4 Multi-year awards | Full credit in the first year, then entered as an active project |
| 4.5 Internal funding as a single band | Needs a decision on whether internal awards should be sized |
| 4.8 Presentation type mixing format and setting | Handled by guidance on the entry form |
| 1.7 Calibration | Still tuned on hypothetical profiles only. A back-test on real past reviews is the most valuable next step |
| Section 7 Workflow | Who enters and verifies data, appeals, record keeping and the use of the result are for the School. The app stores files on the local machine with no login |

## 7. Decisions the committee is asked to make

1. Confirm or replace the three minimums and the 40% standard research effort.
2. Adopt, amend or reject each rule in section 3.
3. Adopt the tier definitions in section 4 and say who assigns tiers.
4. Decide whether the tool informs the chair's evaluation or produces the rating.
5. Approve a back-test on past reviews before first use.
